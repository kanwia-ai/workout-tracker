# Chronic non-specific lower back pain

## Claim

The majority of "lower back pain" in active adults is **non-specific** — no identifiable structural cause on imaging that reliably correlates with symptoms (Hartvigsen 2018 Lancet; Jensen 1994 NEJM; Brinjikji 2015 AJNR). Chronic non-specific LBP is best understood as a **motor-control + capacity problem**, not a tissue-damage problem. The drivers are some combination of:

- Weak posterior chain — especially **glute max + glute medius** (which extend the hip and stabilize the pelvis; when weak, the lumbar erectors take over hip extension and overload).
- **Tight hip flexors** secondary to prolonged sitting — pull the pelvis into anterior tilt, increase lordotic loading.
- Poor **hip hinge mechanics** — lumbar flexion substituted for hip flexion under load.
- Weak **core anti-extension and anti-rotation endurance** (McGill 1999 norms).
- Restricted **ankle dorsiflexion** that drives lumbar compensation in squat patterns.

**The treatment is integration, not avoidance.** Strengthen the system that should be doing the work. Compound lifts — deadlift, squat, hip thrust, RDL — properly patterned ARE the rehab (Berglund 2015; Welch 2021; Owen 2020 network meta-analysis).

## Nuance

- **Chronic ≠ acute.** This entry covers stable, ongoing, manageable pain. For sharp recent-onset pain or a flare-up, see `lower-back-acute` — different programming response (phase down, return gradually).
- **Red flags override.** Bowel/bladder dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, progressive neurological deficit (numbness/weakness in a dermatome), unremitting night pain, fever + back pain, history of cancer — refer out. These are not "non-specific" LBP.
- **The painful muscle is rarely the problem muscle.** The lumbar erectors that feel tight and sore are usually the *symptom* (compensating for weak glutes), not the *cause*. Releasing/stretching them without addressing the glute weakness reproduces the pattern.
- **"Form" is not the holy grail.** The Lancet series and modern literature emphasize that *capacity* (how much load the tissue tolerates) matters more than perfect spinal alignment. Spinal flexion under load isn't categorically dangerous — uncontrolled load relative to capacity is.
- **Directional preference matters early.** Some users feel better with extension (lying prone, McKenzie press-ups); others with flexion (knees to chest). Ask. Bias movements toward the direction that relieves and away from the direction that aggravates during the early weeks of rebuilding.
- **Time of day matters.** Disc hydration is highest in the morning → disc pressure is highest then (Callaghan & McGill 2001 porcine model). Schedule heavy spinal loading away from the first hour after waking when possible.

## What this contradicts

- **"Lower Crossed Syndrome" as a diagnosis.** Janda's framework is observational; the reciprocal-inhibition mechanism is not EMG-supported. Program the constituent interventions (glute work, hip flexor mobility, anti-extension core, hip hinge patterning) without labeling the user with a syndrome. See `myth-postural-syndrome-diagnoses`.
- **"Lifting is bad for your back."** False for chronic non-specific LBP. Progressive resistance training (including deadlifts and squats) is among the most effective interventions per Owen 2020 network meta-analysis; surgery and opioids have weaker evidence (Foster 2018).
- **"You need to stretch your tight back muscles."** Lumbar erector "tightness" is usually compensatory tone from a weak glute pattern. Static stretching the erectors doesn't fix the cause.
- **"Crunches strengthen the core."** Crunches load the worst-case disc mechanic (loaded flexion + repeated cycles). The core's job in LBP rehab is endurance against unwanted motion — anti-flexion (curl-up isometric, dead bug), anti-rotation (Pallof press), anti-lateral-flexion (side plank). See master synthesis "what NOT to codify."

## Application in this app

**Protocol file:** `src/data/rehab-protocols/lower_back.ts` — chronic block.

**Engine behavior when `injuries: [{ part: 'lower_back', severity: 'chronic' }]`:**

1. **Mandatory weekly inclusions:**
   - McGill Big 3 daily-corrective bucket: curl-up (3 × 8s isometric holds), side plank (2 × 30s/side), bird dog (2 × 10/side) — surfaced in warmup or as low-load rest-fillers, not as a separate "rehab day" (master synthesis R5 P15 + R4 P15).
   - **Glute work ≥2×/week** (mandatory per master synthesis R5). Hip thrust, single-leg glute bridge, RDL, banded clamshell/monster walk.
   - Hip hinge dowel drill in warmup on any day featuring loaded hinge.
   - Hip flexor work biased to **activation + length-strengthening first** (banded supine march, eccentric split squat), static stretch as adjunct only — never as the primary intervention (master synthesis R5 P12).
2. **Substitutions (modify, don't omit):**
   - Conventional deadlift → trap-bar deadlift preferred. Trap-bar reduces lumbar shear vs. straight-bar conventional (Swinton 2011 JSCR).
   - Back squat → goblet / front / box squat when technique suspect; full barbell back squat once pain ≤2/10 and pattern is clean.
   - Loaded standing OHP → seated DB OHP if standing aggravates.
3. **Hard bans on chronic:** loaded spinal flexion patterns (Jefferson curl untrained, loaded sit-ups, weighted Russian twist, heavy good-morning), all ballistic spinal rotation. Cap compound RPE ≤7 until criteria pass (Biering-Sørensen ≥60s, resting pain ≤3/10).
4. **Core programming:** anti-flexion / anti-rotation / anti-lateral-flexion only. No crunches, no weighted sit-ups, no loaded Russian twists.
5. **LLM nuance layer must NOT** say: "avoid deadlifts/squats," "your back is fragile," "Lower Crossed Syndrome," "your discs are out of place." It SHOULD say: "we're rebuilding capacity in your hinge pattern with reduced loads," "trap-bar over conventional while we settle the pattern," "your glutes drive hip extension so the spine doesn't have to."
6. **Body-part-to-muscle resolver** (`src/lib/planner/bodyPartMuscleMap.ts`) already maps `lower_back` to `[back, hamstrings, glutes, quads, core, shoulders]` — this is correct and overshoots intentionally so the day-of "anything off today?" affordance flags everything that loads the spine.
