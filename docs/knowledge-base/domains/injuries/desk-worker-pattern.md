# Desk-worker meta-pattern (the constellation, not a syndrome)

## Claim

A high proportion of active adults using this app — software workers, knowledge workers, anyone sitting 6–10 hours/day — present with a recurring **constellation of subclinical patterns** rather than discrete injuries. The components co-occur:

- **Tight / overactive hip flexors** (psoas, iliacus) from prolonged hip flexion in sitting.
- **Weak / inhibited glutes** (max + medius) — underused during sedentary hours; under-trained in a pressing-biased lifting program.
- **Weak posterior chain endurance** — erectors and hamstrings that don't have the capacity for the loaded hinge.
- **Tight pec minor + chest** — from forward-shoulder-rounded posture during typing.
- **Weak rhomboids + mid-trap + lower trap + serratus** — relative inhibition; rarely loaded in everyday life.
- **Overactive upper traps + levator scapulae** — chronic tone holding the head forward.
- **Weak deep neck flexors** — under-trained relative to the constant low-grade load of holding the head against gravity.
- **Restricted ankle dorsiflexion** — calves shortened from prolonged plantar-flexed sitting (especially with shoes that have heel drop).

**The integrated treatment is the program's bias** — not a separate "corrective phase":

- **Posterior-chain-heavy programming.** Glute work ≥2×/week mandatory (master synthesis R5). Hip thrust, RDL, single-leg glute bridge, banded clamshell/monster walk.
- **Hip-hinge patterning** as a foundational skill. Trap-bar deadlift bias for users with any LBP history.
- **1:1 (or 1:1.5) pulling-to-pressing ratio.** Face pull and prone Y as staples; band pull-apart in every upper warmup.
- **Thoracic extension mobility** daily on upper days — foam-roller extensions, open book.
- **Anti-anterior-pelvic-tilt core** — dead bug, McGill curl-up, side plank (anti-extension, anti-rotation, anti-lateral-flexion).
- **Ankle DF mobility** on every lower day — knee-to-wall, half-kneel DF with dowel.
- **Chin tucks paired with scap strengthening** (NOT chin tucks alone).

**The framing matters.** Per master synthesis "what NOT to codify": "Janda lower-crossed / upper-crossed syndrome as diagnosis — reciprocal-inhibition claim not supported by EMG/imaging. Program the constituent interventions, don't label."

Per Hartvigsen 2018 Lancet: low back pain has weak correlation with posture; sticking syndromatic labels on users produces nocebo effects and over-medicalization without improving outcomes.

## Nuance

- **Constellation, not syndrome.** The observation that desk workers commonly have tight hips + weak glutes + tight chest + weak rhomboids + forward head IS real (consistent clinical observation, partial cross-sectional support). The labels ("Lower Crossed Syndrome," "Upper Crossed Syndrome") and their reciprocal-inhibition mechanism are NOT EMG-supported. Describe the pattern; don't diagnose the syndrome.
- **Not every desk worker has all of it.** Some have hip flexor tightness without glute weakness; some have shoulder issues without ankle restriction. Screen the user, treat what's present.
- **The training program IS the rehab.** Master synthesis R4 P15 + R5 P15: rehab is WOVEN into sessions — no standalone "rehab day." Glute activation in the warmup, hip hinge dowel before deadlift, face pull as staple, thoracic extension on upper days, dead bug in core work.
- **Exposure modification is real but limited.** Standing desks, posture breaks, walking meetings help marginally. They don't replace strength training. The hour at the gym matters more than the 9 hours at the desk — when the gym hour is dosed correctly.
- **Don't catastrophize.** "Sitting is the new smoking" framing produces fear-avoidance. Sitting + lifting 3×/week is a different exposure profile than sitting alone.

## What this contradicts

- **"You have Lower Crossed Syndrome / Upper Crossed Syndrome."** The constellation is observable; the named syndromes with their reciprocal-inhibition mechanism are not EMG-supported. Program the constituents; don't apply the label. See `myth-postural-syndrome-diagnoses`.
- **"You need to do correctives before strength training."** False dichotomy. The strength program IS the corrective when designed with the right bias (glute volume, pulling volume, hip hinge, dead bug). Master synthesis R4 P15 + R5 P15 explicit.
- **"Stretch your tight muscles to fix posture."** Static stretching alone doesn't reshape the pattern. Strengthening the underactive partners does. Per master synthesis "what NOT to codify": "Static stretching tight muscles as primary treatment for 'tight' hip flexors or upper traps — these are usually lengthened-weak or overactive, not short."
- **"Posture braces fix posture."** Bracing produces no durable change. Strengthening + exposure modification do.

## Application in this app

**Protocol files (cross-cutting):**
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/lower_back.ts` (chronic block) — handles the LBP component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/hip_flexors.ts` (chronic block) — handles the tight-hip-flexor component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/upper_back.ts` (chronic block) — handles the thoracic-kyphosis / mid-trap component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/trap.ts` (chronic block) — handles the upper-trap-dominance component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/neck.ts` (chronic block) — handles the forward-head component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/ankle.ts` (chronic block) — handles the DF deficit component.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/shoulder.ts` (chronic block) — handles overlap with shoulder impingement.

**Engine behavior — the desk-worker overlay** (when user reports sedentary occupation OR posture_notes mentioning desk/computer work OR has 2+ flags from {lower_back, hip_flexors, upper_back, neck, trap, ankle}):

1. **Programming bias:**
   - Pulling sets ≥ pressing sets weekly (1:1 minimum, 1.5:1 during active correction).
   - Glute work ≥2×/week mandatory.
   - Hip hinge dowel drill in warmup on any day with loaded hinge.
   - Trap-bar deadlift preferred over conventional during early weeks if any LBP history.
   - Face pull and band pull-apart as staples (every upper-body warmup).
2. **Warmup overlay on every session:**
   - Upper days: thoracic extension foam roll, band pull-apart, chin tuck + scap pair.
   - Lower days: glute activation (banded clamshell or monster walk), hip flexor 90/90 or half-kneel hip flexor with PPT cue, ankle DF mob.
3. **Cooldown / mobility tab:** static hip flexor stretch (couch / kneeling), doorway pec stretch — adjunct, not primary. Per `static-vs-dynamic-stretching` KB entry, these belong in cooldown.
4. **Core programming:** anti-flexion / anti-rotation / anti-lateral-flexion only — dead bug, McGill curl-up, side plank, Pallof press. No crunches / sit-ups / Russian twists.
5. **Onboarding:** capture sedentary hours / desk work and forward-head/rounded-shoulder self-assessment as inputs. Surface "desk-day pattern" language; DO NOT use "Lower Crossed" / "Upper Crossed Syndrome" labels in any user-facing copy.
6. **LLM nuance layer must NOT** say: "you have Lower Crossed Syndrome," "you have Upper Crossed Syndrome," "sitting is destroying your body," "you need correctives before strength work." It SHOULD say: "we're biasing your program toward what desk work under-trains — glutes, mid-back, deep neck flexors, ankle mobility. The corrections live inside your strength sessions, not as a separate routine. The static stretches are in cooldown."
7. **Time horizon framing:** this is a 6–12 week reshape, not a one-session fix. Communicate that in onboarding ("we're playing the long game").
8. **Cross-link with `myth-postural-syndrome-diagnoses`** — the LLM SHOULD validate the user's pattern observation if they bring up "Lower Crossed Syndrome" from elsewhere, but reframe the language while preserving the interventions.
