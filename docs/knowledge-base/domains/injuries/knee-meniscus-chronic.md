# Chronic meniscus / knee pattern (stable, manageable)

## Claim

A chronic, stable knee or meniscus issue — degenerative meniscal change, knee osteoarthritis, long-standing patellofemoral pain (PFP), or post-conservative-management meniscus tear — responds best to **progressive loaded exercise**, not avoidance. Two landmark trials (Kise 2016 OMEX, BMJ; van de Graaf 2022 ESCAPE 5-year, JAMA Netw Open) showed exercise therapy is **non-inferior to arthroscopic partial meniscectomy** for degenerative meniscal tear over multi-year follow-up. The 10-year OMEX follow-up sustained that finding.

The drivers in the chronic pattern:

- **Weak hip abductors / external rotators** (especially glute medius) → frontal-plane knee collapse (valgus) under load → patellofemoral compression and meniscal shearing (Powers 2010; Nascimento 2018 meta-analysis showing hip+knee > knee alone).
- **Quad weakness** (especially after any period of disuse; quad atrophy is documented post-knee injury) → reduced shock absorption, increased patellofemoral joint reaction force.
- **Restricted ankle dorsiflexion** → forces forward knee translation OR forces lumbar/torso compensation; Macrum 2012 showed limited DF causes increased valgus + reduced quad activation in squats.
- **Tight hip flexors / weak glutes** (the desk-worker pattern overlay) reinforcing the valgus-prone position.

**The rehab is integration:** glute med strengthening (banded clamshell, monster walk, side-lying hip abduction), ankle dorsiflexion mobility (knee-to-wall, soleus-bent-knee stretch), TKE (terminal knee extension) for VMO-area quad recruitment, hip thrust + RDL + step-up for posterior chain.

**Reverse incline walking** (treadmill at ~10% incline, walking forward — NOT backwards; "reverse incline" is the modality name preserved in the codified protocol) is a real post-op modality that loads the patellar tendon and quads at low joint shear. It is preserved in `meniscus.ts` and `knee_pfp.ts`.

**BFR (Blood Flow Restriction)** training is a legitimate option when high-load knee work is contraindicated. Patterson 2019 position stand: low-load (~20–30% 1RM) with cuff pressure produces hypertrophy comparable to high-load training in many populations — useful in the chronic-knee user who can't load heavy yet.

## Nuance

- **"VMO isolation" is overclaimed.** The vastus medialis obliquus does not work independently of the rest of the quad — you can bias its activation slightly with terminal knee extension and adduction-cue squats, but you cannot isolate it. Pop fitness culture's "VMO drops" narrative oversells the effect. Frame as "quad work with terminal-knee-extension emphasis" — same exercise, honest framing.
- **Chronic ≠ acute.** This entry covers stable, manageable pain. Catching, locking, true giving-way, or sudden effusion = different category; see `knee-meniscus-acute`.
- **Imaging findings poorly correlate with symptoms in the over-40 knee.** Many asymptomatic adults have degenerative meniscal changes on MRI; symptoms drive treatment, not images.
- **Cartilage doesn't degenerate from training.** Bricca 2019 systematic review: exercise either preserves or improves cartilage health in at-risk and OA knees. The fear that "squats wear out cartilage" is not supported.
- **Full-depth squats are not categorically dangerous on a chronic knee.** Hartmann 2013 review found no evidence that deep squat is more injurious than parallel when properly loaded. It's load × depth × pattern that matters, not depth alone. Master synthesis explicitly flags "fear-avoidance messaging for deep squats on chronic-knee user" as something NOT to codify.
- **Pivot-and-cut sports are the higher-risk loading.** Axial-load squat is much safer per unit of muscle work than ballistic pivot loading for a degenerative meniscus.

## What this contradicts

- **"Surgery is the only option for a meniscus tear."** OMEX and ESCAPE both showed exercise is non-inferior over multi-year follow-up. Surgery has a role for true mechanical symptoms (locking) but not for routine degenerative tears with pain.
- **"Deep squats wear out your knees."** Not load-dependent at submaximal weights; Bricca 2019 + Hartmann 2013 contradict the avoidance narrative.
- **"You need to isolate the VMO."** EMG and biomechanical literature: VMO cannot be functionally isolated from the rest of the quad. Bias is possible; isolation is not.
- **"Avoid squats / lunges / knee bending."** Avoidance entrenches the weakness that drives the pattern. The lift stays in; the load, depth, or variant changes (master synthesis R4 P5).

## Application in this app

**Protocol files:**
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/meniscus.ts` — degenerative / post-conservative meniscus.
- `src/data/rehab-protocols/knee_pfp.ts` — patellofemoral pain syndrome.

**Engine behavior when `injuries: [{ part: 'left_meniscus' | 'right_meniscus' | 'left_knee' | 'right_knee', severity: 'chronic' }]`:**

1. **Mandatory weekly inclusions (master synthesis R4):**
   - ≥1 direct quad-loading exercise/week (squat variant, leg press 0–60°, or split-squat).
   - ≥2 posterolateral-hip exercises/week (hip thrust, clamshell, banded monster walk, side-lying hip abduction, single-leg RDL).
2. **Daily-bucket vs heavy-bucket split:**
   - Low-load rehab items (TKE, quad set, clamshell, glute bridge, standing hip abduction) can appear every session.
   - Heavy knee work capped at 3/week with ≥48h recovery between heavy sessions.
3. **Substitutions (modify, don't omit):**
   - Forward lunge → reverse lunge (lower patellofemoral compression at the same intensity per Riemann 2011).
   - Step-down (high box) → lateral step-up (less knee flexion under load).
   - Walking lunge loaded → split squat or reverse lunge.
   - Full-ATG back squat heavy → front squat or heel-elevated back squat moderate.
4. **Hip-mechanics-first sequencing:** glute med activation (clamshell, banded lateral walk) appears in the warmup BEFORE the main squat/hinge lift on lower days.
5. **Ankle mobility on every squat day** (`src/data/rehab-protocols/ankle.ts` overlay): half-kneel DF mob with dowel, knee-to-wall, soleus stretch.
6. **Analgesic primer:** if user self-reports knee pain ≥4/10 on a given day, Pass 5 validation injects Spanish squat 5 × 30–45s or wall-sit 5 × 30–45s as the session's first exercise (master synthesis R4; based on Rio 2015 isometric analgesia in patellar tendinopathy + Basas case series).
7. **Body-part-to-muscle resolver** maps `left_meniscus | right_meniscus | left_knee | right_knee` to `[quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves]` — correct.
8. **LLM nuance layer must NOT** say: "you need surgery," "your meniscus is torn so avoid squats," "deep squats are dangerous." It SHOULD say: "we're keeping the squat in with a hip-first warmup, modifying depth/variant for the first weeks, and progressing back to full depth as the pattern cleans up," "glute med work is the priority because it controls how your knee tracks under load."
9. **BFR is optional, gated behind equipment availability.** If the user has BFR cuffs (uncommon), the LLM may surface it as a low-load hypertrophy option for the affected leg. Don't surface BFR for users without the equipment.
