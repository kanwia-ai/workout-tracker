# Mind-muscle connection — internal vs external focus, the research

## Claim

Internal attentional focus ("squeeze the muscle, feel the contraction in the pec") and external attentional focus ("push the bar away as hard as possible") produce **different stimulus profiles** during resistance training. The evidence:

1. **Schoenfeld 2018** — 8-week RCT, trained college-aged men, equal-volume biceps and quad training. The internal-focus group (told to "squeeze the muscle as you lift") gained **more biceps thickness** than the external-focus group (told to "lift the weight"). Quad hypertrophy did not differ. Effect was muscle-specific: smaller muscles with strong proprioceptive feedback (biceps) responded; larger compound-driven muscles (quad in squat) did not.

2. **Calatayud 2016** — acute EMG study, bench press at 50% and 80% 1RM. Internal-focus cueing produced **higher pec and triceps EMG activity** than external-focus cueing at 50% 1RM. At 80% 1RM the effect disappeared — at heavy loads, the body recruits maximally regardless of attentional focus, because the load *forces* the recruitment.

3. **Calatayud 2017** — follow-up across training experience. Effect of internal focus on EMG was stronger in trained vs untrained lifters. Experience builds the kinesthetic map.

4. **Snyder & Leech 2009** — single instructional intervention ("think about pulling with your lats, not your arms") significantly increased lat EMG during the lat pulldown. One coaching cue moved the needle measurably.

**Practical synthesis:** internal focus matters most for (a) smaller / harder-to-feel muscles, (b) sub-maximal loads (where you can choose recruitment patterns), and (c) trained lifters who can leverage the existing kinesthetic awareness. External focus matters most for (a) heavy compounds where the goal is maximal force / 1RM (b) skill-based movements (Olympic lifts) where over-internalizing can disrupt timing.

The trainer's framing (philosophy doc §7): *"More warmup sets, sometimes a machine version first to teach the body the pattern."* Warming up is itself a mind-muscle teaching tool — the lifter rehearses recruitment at progressively higher loads until the target muscle is online. This is research-supported. (Calatayud 2016 found EMG differences at sub-max loads, exactly where warmups operate.)

## Nuance

- **The effect is muscle-specific, not universal.** Schoenfeld 2018 found NO effect on quad hypertrophy. The quad in a squat is so anatomically obligated (and the load so demanding) that "thinking about the quads" doesn't change recruitment meaningfully. Biceps in a curl is the opposite — the bicep is one of two muscles that could do the work (vs forearms), and the attentional focus moves the dial.
- **The effect drops off at high intensities.** Calatayud 2016: at 80% 1RM, internal vs external focus didn't differ in EMG. The intensity *is* the cue at that point. Implication: cue "squeeze the muscle" on warmup and accessory sets. On a max effort, the lifter shouldn't be busy thinking — they should be pushing.
- **"Mind-muscle connection" is not a synonym for "lift slowly with light weight."** The Schoenfeld 2018 protocol used standard loads (8-12 reps near failure). Internal focus is a *cueing strategy*, not a tempo or load prescription. Don't conflate them.
- **The effect is training-experience-dependent.** Calatayud 2017: trained lifters benefit more from internal focus than novices. Novices need to learn the pattern first (motor skill); the kinesthetic awareness comes later. This validates the trainer's progression: machine first (teach the pattern), free weight later (now you can feel it).
- **For strength-only goals (1RM-focused), external focus likely wins.** Wulf 2013 and the motor-learning literature broadly favor external focus for skill execution and maximal force production. Translation: a powerlifter on a heavy single shouldn't be thinking "squeeze the quads" — they should be thinking "push the floor away." For our app's user base (hypertrophy / fat loss / general fitness goals), internal focus is the lever.

## What this contradicts

- **"Mind-muscle connection is bro-science with no research support."** False. Schoenfeld 2018 + Calatayud 2016 are RCTs / EMG-controlled designs in peer-reviewed journals. The effect is real, muscle-specific, and intensity-dependent.
- **"Internal focus reduces strength gains."** Not at hypertrophy intensities (8-12 reps). At max efforts (1-3 reps), external focus is better for force expression — but that's a strength-peaking context, not a build-muscle context.
- **"Just move the weight; the muscle will grow."** True only at maximal intensities and only for muscles that are anatomically obligated to do the work. For smaller / harder-to-feel muscles at moderate loads, attentional focus measurably changes recruitment and downstream hypertrophy.

## Application in this app

- **Per-exercise cue** from [hard-to-feel-exercises-catalog](hard-to-feel-exercises-catalog.md) is rendered as the primary content on the exercise info sheet. This is the internal-focus cue — short, specific, anatomically targeted ("drive elbows down and back," "pinch a pencil between your shoulder blades").
- **Warmup framing:** the LLM nuance copy may explain warmups as *both* movement preparation *and* mind-muscle rehearsal — "the first warmup set is to remind your back that it's the muscle doing the work, not your arms." This is research-supported (Snyder 2009 single-cue intervention).
- **Mind-muscle tap** (felt / didn't feel it) feeds the engine's per-user model of which exercises this user struggles to connect to. The tap is the lifter's self-report of whether internal focus was achieved.
- **Cue persistence:** the same cue is shown every time the user does the exercise until they consistently report `'felt'`. Cue rotation is *noise* in the early connection-building phase. Only after `'felt'` is logged 3+ times should the engine consider rotating cues for variety / different angles.
- **LLM is NOT allowed** to invent novel cues for exercises in `HARD_TO_FEEL_EXERCISE_IDS`. It must use the cue from this KB. Inventing a new cue every session is the AI failure mode this entry exists to prevent.
- **For heavy compound main lifts (>80% 1RM territory):** the LLM nuance copy should default to external-focus cueing ("drive the floor away" on squat, "push the bar to the ceiling" on bench). Internal-focus cueing on a 5RM squat is counter-productive.
