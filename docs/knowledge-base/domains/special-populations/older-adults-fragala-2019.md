# Older adults (60+) — train near failure, the contraindications are NOT age itself

## Claim

The Fragala 2019 NSCA position statement on resistance training for older adults is unambiguous: **healthy older adults can and should perform progressive resistance training with moderate-to-high loads (60–85% 1RM), close to failure, to maximize hypertrophy and strength outcomes.** Age alone is NOT a contraindication to near-failure training, and blanket RIR caps based on chronological age contradict the position-statement-level consensus.

Key Fragala 2019 recommendations relevant to the engine:
- **Load:** moderate-to-high (60–85% 1RM) for both hypertrophy and strength outcomes; very high (>85% 1RM) is appropriate for advanced older lifters seeking strength specifically.
- **Volume:** 2–4 sets per exercise, 6–12 exercises per session, 2–3 sessions/week for the same muscle group — the same general envelope as for younger trained adults.
- **Effort:** "near-maximal" intensity (i.e., near-failure, RIR 1–2) is endorsed for hypertrophy outcomes in this population. The position statement does NOT recommend a population-wide RIR floor of 2+ based on age alone.
- **Power training (moderate loads moved with intent)** is particularly valuable for fall prevention, gait speed, and functional outcomes — Fragala 2019 dedicates substantial attention to this. This is in ADDITION to (not in place of) traditional hypertrophy/strength work.
- **Frequency:** 2–3 sessions/week minimum; up to 3–4 sessions/week for trained older adults pursuing strength/hypertrophy outcomes.

Supporting evidence:
- **Borde 2015** (Sports Med dose-response meta-analysis, healthy older adults, n = ~3300): higher intensity (≥80% 1RM) produced significantly greater strength gains than lower intensity. Hypertrophy responses also dose-responded to volume and intensity within the same envelope as in younger trained populations.
- **Peterson 2011** (MSSE meta-analysis of resistance exercise on lean body mass in aging adults): consistent, dose-responsive lean-mass gains across the 50–80 age range with progressive resistance training.
- **Csapo & Alegre 2016** (meta-analysis comparing moderate vs heavy loads in elderly): heavy loads (≥80% 1RM) produced greater strength gains than moderate loads; hypertrophy outcomes were similar across loads when total volume was matched (same load-spectrum principle as in younger populations — see `hypertrophy-rep-ranges`).

**The "older adults should stick to light weights / high reps" recommendation is myth, not research.** The position-statement-level evidence supports training older adults closer to how we train younger trained adults, not the inverse.

## Nuance

- **The actual contraindications are health-status driven, not age-driven.** Uncontrolled cardiovascular disease (uncontrolled hypertension >180/110, recent MI, unstable angina), recent surgery, acute orthopedic injury, balance disorders, advanced osteoporosis with prior fracture, prescription medications that affect HR/BP/balance (beta-blockers, alpha-blockers, etc.) — these are the conditions that warrant intensity moderation or medical clearance. Chronological age by itself is not. See `chronic-conditions-meta` for the umbrella entry.

- **Recovery between heavy sets typically warrants more time.** Fragala 2019 recommends longer rest intervals (2–3+ minutes for compound work) for older adults seeking strength outcomes. The base rest-period table in this app (180s compound / 120s accessory / 75s isolation) already accommodates this — there is no need for additional age-based rest extensions.

- **Warmup matters more.** Joint mobility and tendon adaptability decline with age; longer warmups (extra mobility moves, additional ramp sets on first heavy lift) reduce tweak risk. The current prompt's "45–60 → pad warm-ups by 1 extra mobility move per session" overlay is consistent with this.

- **Protein intake should be higher than for younger adults.** PROT-AGE position paper (Bauer 2013): **≥1.0–1.2 g/kg/day** for healthy older adults, **≥1.2–1.5 g/kg/day** during illness/injury or active training. Morton 2018 meta-analysis: protein supplementation augments resistance training adaptations with the effect more pronounced in older adults (likely because baseline intake is more often inadequate). For older adults strength-training, a practical floor is **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, distributed across 3–4 meals with ≥30 g protein per meal to overcome the age-related anabolic resistance.

- **High-impact movements remain a programming variable.** Plyometrics, jumping, depth jumps, deep box jumps — these have a higher orthopedic risk profile in older lifters, especially those with osteoarthritis or osteoporosis. The current prompt's "60+ → replace plyometric or jumping movements with a low-impact equivalent" overlay is correct. Heavy compound lifting (squat, deadlift, bench press, row) is NOT in this category — it remains appropriate and beneficial.

- **The "70+ frail" sub-population is different.** Fragala 2019 distinguishes between healthy older adults and frail/sarcopenic older adults. The latter benefit from slightly higher rep ranges (8–15) initially while strength baseline is built, but the trajectory is STILL toward higher loads as tolerated — not a permanent prescription of "light weights forever." The current app's onboarding does not distinguish frailty status; if a 70+ user reports significant deconditioning, the LLM should treat them more like a novice (lower starting volume, longer LP runway) rather than as "frail" per se.

- **Per-individual variation is large.** A 70-year-old recreational lifter with 30 years of training history is closer to an intermediate male/female adult than to a sedentary 70-year-old. Use `training_age_months` and `injuries` as the primary gating signals; age is a secondary input.

## What this contradicts

- **"After 60, switch to light weights and high reps to be safe."** Comprehensively refuted by Fragala 2019, Borde 2015, Csapo 2016. (Myth entry: `older-adults-shouldnt-lift-heavy`.)

- **"Older adults shouldn't train near failure."** Fragala 2019 explicitly endorses near-maximal training for hypertrophy outcomes in this population. Blanket RIR floors of 2 or 3 based on chronological age alone are over-conservative and counterproductive — they under-stimulate the muscle and accelerate sarcopenia.

- **"Older adults are too fragile for compound lifts."** False as a blanket claim. Heavy compounds (squat, deadlift, bench, row) are the most muscle-, strength-, and bone-density-protective movements available — Watson 2018 LIFTMOR (in postmenopausal women) and Fragala 2019 in older adults broadly. The contraindications are condition-specific (acute lumbar disc, hip OA with severe pain, etc.), not age-categorical.

## What this contradicts in the engine

- The current `generatePlan.ts` line 326 overlay (`60+ → same RIR prescription as the general population (1-3) UNLESS the user has flagged a contraindication`) is correctly aligned with Fragala 2019 and this entry. The legacy "cap RIR at 2 on ALL sets" rule was already revised per the myth sweep — keep it that way.

- The prompt's age-based overlay at line 326 should NOT regress to a blanket RIR floor. Future edits that add "60+ → RIR ≥ 2 on all sets" must be rejected — they contradict the position-statement-level research.

## Application in this app

- **Engine intensity prescription for 60+ users:** same RIR window as general adult population (1–3), unless the user has flagged a contraindication via injuries or (eventually) chronic-condition fields. The "protected by age" framing is a stereotype and the engine correctly does not impose it.

- **Engine modifications for 60+ users:**
  - Replace plyometric / jumping movements with low-impact equivalents from the pool (current overlay; keep).
  - Pad warmups with one additional mobility move per session for 45–60 users (current overlay; keep — and arguably extend to 60+).
  - Prioritize compound multi-joint work for the strength + bone-density + sarcopenia-protection benefit.
  - Sufficient rest between heavy sets is already in the base table (compound = 180s).

- **LLM nuance layer for 60+ users:**
  - Cite Fragala 2019 when explaining "yes, you can still train heavy / near-failure" — this is one of the highest-confidence claims in the older-adult literature.
  - When a 60+ user expresses reluctance to train heavy, acknowledge the safety reasoning (real for many older adults) and route to "we'll progress conservatively but we're not capping you at light weights forever — that would accelerate sarcopenia, which is what we're protecting against."
  - Surface the protein floor (≥1.2–1.6 g/kg/day) for older adults in plan rationale at least once per mesocycle — see `protein-requirements-for-muscle-preservation`.
  - When the user reports a condition that IS a real contraindication (cardiovascular disease, recent surgery, acute injury, balance disorder, prescription affecting HR), the LLM must surface a "consult your doctor" disclaimer and NOT pretend the program is medically tailored. See `chronic-conditions-meta`.

- **Onboarding affordance gap:** the app does not currently capture chronic-condition status (cardiovascular, metabolic, autoimmune, recent surgery) or current medications. For 60+ users especially, this is a meaningful gap. See `chronic-conditions-meta` for the recommended affordance.
