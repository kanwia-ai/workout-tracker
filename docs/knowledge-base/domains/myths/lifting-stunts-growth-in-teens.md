# Myth: Lifting weights stunts growth in teenagers / damages growth plates

## The myth (verbatim)
"Don't let kids lift weights — it'll stunt their growth." "Heavy lifting damages the growth plates." "Kids should wait until they're done growing to start training."

## Why the myth persists
- A small number of historical case reports of growth-plate injuries in youth weightlifters got generalized into a blanket prohibition.
- The injuries in those reports were associated with unsupervised use of inappropriately heavy loads, ballistic/uncontrolled lifts, and bad technique — not with supervised, properly-loaded resistance training.
- Cultural and parental conservatism around children + heavy objects produces a strong default to "wait until they're older."

## What the research actually says
1. **Faigenbaum 2009** (NSCA position statement on youth resistance training): well-designed, supervised resistance training programs do NOT have negative effects on growth plates, linear growth, cardiovascular health, or development. The position statement explicitly retired the "stunts growth" myth.
2. **Lloyd & Faigenbaum 2014** International Consensus position statement: endorsed by major pediatric and sports-medicine organizations. Youth resistance training is safe and beneficial when appropriately programmed.
3. **Behringer 2010** meta-analysis (Pediatrics): resistance training in children and adolescents produces strength gains, improves motor performance, and is safe. Injury rates are LOWER than in many youth sports.
4. **The injuries that DO occur** in youth resistance training are typically: dropping a barbell on themselves, attempting 1RM lifts without spotters, sumo deadlifting at inappropriate loads, and similar supervision failures — not from the training stimulus damaging growth plates.

## The corrected understanding
- Resistance training is safe and beneficial for children and adolescents when programmed appropriately (proper progression, supervision, technique focus, age-appropriate loads).
- Growth plates are NOT specifically vulnerable to controlled resistance training. They are vulnerable to acute trauma — which applies to any sport.
- Starting resistance training before puberty does NOT impair growth or cause early closure of growth plates.
- The age-appropriate program emphasizes movement skill, technique, body-weight + light-load work, and supervision — not maximal loads.
