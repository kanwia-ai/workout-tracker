# Myth: Light weights for tone, heavy weights for bulk

## The myth (verbatim)
"Light weights + high reps = toning. Heavy weights + low reps = bulk."

This is the combined / compound version of [high-reps-for-tone](high-reps-for-tone.md) + [low-reps-make-women-bulky](low-reps-make-women-bulky.md). Cross-reference both entries for the underlying mechanisms.

## Why the myth persists
- It pairs into a clean rule-of-thumb that maps onto male/female stereotypes about training goals.
- Group-fitness culture and gym marketing have institutionalized it (women's classes use light weights; "bulking" classes use heavy weights).
- The dichotomy is rhetorically powerful even though both halves are wrong.

## What the research actually says
- Rep range does NOT determine "toned vs bulky" appearance. Both light and heavy loading produce hypertrophy when sets are taken close to failure (Schoenfeld 2017).
- Visible body composition is driven by total muscle × body-fat percentage, not by rep range.
- "Bulky" requires deliberate surplus + years of training and is hard for most people regardless of rep range (Hagstrom 2020 for women specifically; same applies to men in different magnitudes).

## The corrected understanding
- Rep range is a programming variable for managing fatigue, time efficiency, and connective-tissue stress. It's not a body-shape lever.
- The "toned" look = enough muscle + low enough body fat.
- The "bulky" look = a lot of muscle (years of work) + the body fat % at which it shows.
- Pick rep range based on training age, equipment, recovery needs, time available — NOT based on the imagined aesthetic outcome.
